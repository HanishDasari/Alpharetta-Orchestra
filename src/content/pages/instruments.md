A quality instrument is essential. Poor quality instruments cause
frustration for the student and prevent adequate progress.

Be very cautious of cheap instruments sold online — often called
"V.S.O.s," or Violin Shaped Objects. They frequently need repairs that cost
more than the instrument did. If the price sounds too good to be true, it
probably is.

## Questions to ask before you rent

- What is the rental schedule — monthly, quarterly, or something else?
- Does **100%** of what you pay in rent apply toward an eventual purchase?
- Can you trade up to a larger size as your child grows?
- Is insurance included, and what exactly does it cover?
- Which repairs are covered under the rental agreement?

## What a good shop provides

- Brand-name strings
- On-site setup of bridge, soundpost, and fittings, so the instrument is
  ready to play when you get it
- A real horsehair bow, already rosined
- Qualified luthiers doing repairs in house
- Regular visits to the school

## Accessories every student needs

- A cleaning cloth and rosin
- A rigid shoulder rest — violin and viola
- A rock stop — cello and bass
